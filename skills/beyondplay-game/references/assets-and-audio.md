# Assets and audio

Sources: the creator terms (assets, music, AI disclosure), the platform's
asset pipelines (3D, audio, image generation) and the published games'
credits.

## Policy in one paragraph

Use assets you have the right to put in a commercial game: your own work,
public domain (CC0), or a licence that allows paid and ad-supported games.
Music follows the same rule. Name every third-party asset and track in a
`CREDITS.md` (title, author, source, licence); the creator attests to the
list on submission and carries the liability under the creator terms. CC0 is
the default, because it needs nothing verified. Try procedural sound first;
it costs nothing, weighs almost nothing and needs no licence. Generated assets follow
the generator policy below: platform-provided within a quota, bring your own
key, or free. Record every asset's provenance (made, generated by which
model, or taken from which library under which licence), because the
platform tags AI-generated content and reviewers check it.

## Free libraries (by name, with licence notes)

| Library | What | Licence | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kenney | 2D and 3D asset packs, UI, audio | CC0; attribution not required | The safest default. |
| Poly Pizza | 10,000+ low-poly 3D models | Mixed CC0 and CC-BY | Filter to CC0. The exact split and API limits are unverified. |
| Freesound | 738,000+ sounds | CC0, CC-BY, CC-BY-NC | Use CC0 items only. The API is free for non-commercial use only, so download by hand. |
| Sketchfab | 3D models | Filter to CC0 | A weaker long-term dependency after its 2026 sale. |
| OpenGameArt | 2D, 3D, audio | Mixed | Use CC0 items. Its credit format is the one to copy for any CC-BY item: `[name] by [author] licensed [licence]: [url]`. |
| Quaternius | Low-poly 3D | Free for commercial games; no redistribution of raw files | Fine inside a game; do not re-host the raw files. |
| Mixamo | Humanoid clips and auto-rig | Free for commercial use; no redistribution of raw files; needs an Adobe login | Upload a single joined mesh in rest pose (FBX, embedded textures, about 1.8 m). |

CC-BY items need a credit; CC-BY-NC items are not usable. Keep every credit
in the game's `CREDITS.md`.

## Music

- The rule (founder decision, 2026-10-24): any track the creator owns or
  holds a commercial licence for, named in `CREDITS.md` with its source and
  licence. Not allowed: tracks ripped from other games, videos or streaming
  services; licences that exclude games or monetised use (CC-BY-NC, most
  personal-use and self-serve generator terms); tracks whose licence you
  cannot show. A takedown on a track removes that game.
- CC0 is the default. The published games use CC0 loops, cut to a clean
  loop point and credited in `CREDITS.md`.
- The Pixabay Content License (commercial use including games, no credit
  required) is acceptable under the rule; BullRun ships one such track.
  Pixabay pages cannot be downloaded from a cloud session; a person
  downloads the file. Check the track page for a Content ID note before using
  the track in posted video.
- Generated music is not available as a platform feature: the Eleven Music
  terms exclude monetised games on self-serve plans, forbid building a music
  library and forbid resale, so an Enterprise agreement must come first. Suno
  and Udio have no API and are bring-your-own-subscription upload tools.
- Loops: take the downbeat from the drop's envelope, not from a spectral-flux
  comb (it locks onto off-beat hats); pick loop points by bar self-similarity.
  Decoded MP3s run about 40 ms shorter than their metadata says; match within
  about 0.07 s.
- Mix by measurement (per-event RMS against the music), not by asking a
  language model to listen; in A/B listening tests it picked whichever clip
  came second.

## Sound effects

- Procedural first. `zzfx` (MIT, under 1 KB compressed) is already a root
  dependency; `jsfxr` is public domain. A tiny WebAudio synth in
  `src/audio.ts`, unlocked on the first `pointerdown`, is the pattern several
  games use.
- Recorded or generated SFX are a step up, not the default. Generated SFX
  (ElevenLabs, about $0.02 each) are allowed under the generator policy; keep
  raw takes out of the bundle and ship only the clips played.
- Phones reproduce little below about 150 Hz, and generated beams and loops
  tend to come back as all hiss (above 6 kHz) or all sub-bass; pitch bass
  takes up into 150 to 600 Hz, low-pass the hiss, and add one-shot impacts
  for hit feel.
- Ship only the clips the game plays (BullRun cut 3.6 MB to 277 KB). Clips
  under 2 KB are inlined as `data:` URLs; decode them in place because the
  production CSP refuses `fetch()` of a `data:` URL.
- Never create an `AudioContext` before a gesture. Give the game a mute
  toggle.

## Generator policy: platform-provided within quota, bring your own key, or free

| Tier | Who | What | Cost to the creator |
|---|---|---|---|
| Free | everyone | CC0 libraries, own assets, procedural audio | none |
| Platform-provided within quota | verified creators (accepted terms, declared age, verified email) | proposed monthly allowance: 300 Meshy credits (about 10 textured meshes, 3 per day), 2 rig-plus-animate jobs, 50 sound effects, 20 images; a hard stop at zero | none |
| Bring your own key | verified creators with their own Meshy, ElevenLabs or Gemini key | the provider's plan limits; no fallback to platform keys; outputs still moderated and tagged | the creator's own bill |

These tiers and numbers are the platform report's recommendation, not yet a
shipped feature. Today the repository's generation scripts read keys from the
environment (`MESHY_API_KEY`, `GOOGLE_GENERATIVE_AI_API_KEY`); an agent
without those keys works in the free tier.

Reference costs per asset, so you can judge a request: a textured Meshy mesh
is 30 credits, a rigged character with three clips about 44 credits, an
ElevenLabs sound effect about $0.02, a Gemini image $0.034 to $0.067.

Rules that apply to every generated asset:

- Store the output in the game folder at once; Meshy deletes API outputs
  after 3 days on most plans.
- Record provenance and disclose AI involvement; the platform tags generated
  assets and store policies require disclosure.
- Generated content is moderated and reviewed like any other asset.
- Music is never generated (see above).

## 3D pipeline notes

- Character from an idea: reference image (front view, neutral pose, plain
  white background, no accessories) to Meshy image-to-3D (`should_remesh`,
  `target_polycount` about 14,000 for a body and 3,000 for props,
  `pose_mode: a-pose`), then Meshy auto-rig (its walk and run clips are human
  motions; pose procedurally instead), then wearables made the same way and
  parented to bones.
- Optimise every GLB: `gltf-transform optimize --texture-compress webp
  --texture-size 1024 --compress meshopt --simplify false --instance false
  --flatten false --join false` (flatten and join detach bone-parented
  props). Results seen: an ape at 33 MB became 974 KB; a bull at 5.1 MB became
  1.9 MB.
- Meshy wires the texture into emissive at full strength; clamp emissive to
  about 0.5.
- No Draco (its decoder runs in a blob worker that strict CSPs block); use
  meshopt. Register a `createImageBitmap` `EXT_texture_webp` plugin or
  textures render grey on iPhone.
- Budget: at most 60 draw calls and 300k triangles with the character loaded;
  small parts at 512 px textures; decimate heavy accessories.
- Procedural poses over clips: restore the clip pose before each mixer update
  or layered bends accumulate on held frames. Do not sell speed with a run
  clip faster than about 1.5x; exaggerate the pose instead.
- Free image-to-3D (TRELLIS, Hunyuan3D on Hugging Face Spaces) works but
  anonymous GPU quota runs out; Meshy is the reliable paid path.

## Images, icon and banner

- The icon (512 x 512, `assets/icon.png`) is required; publishing refuses a
  game without one. The banner (1920 x 1080, `assets/banner.jpg`) is
  optional. Their sources are plain HTML canvases in `assets/`, rendered by
  `npm run render-icon` and `npm run render-banner`, so they live in git and
  re-render deterministically. No external files inside them beyond a Google
  webfont; embed pixel art as inline data URLs.
- The banner is the social preview (`og:image`, `twitter:image`) and the
  Telegram duel card; the title must not cover the character.
- Key art with Gemini (`gemini-3-pro-image`): always send a character
  reference render so the art stays on-model; supported aspects include 1:1,
  16:9 and 21:9, not 2:1; a spend cap returns 429, so fall back to in-engine
  renders and regenerate later.
- Import sprites from `src/` so the bundler content-hashes them; fixed
  filenames under `public/` can serve stale from the edge cache for up to
  seven days.
- Keep end-card art small (about 60 KB webp or png).

## Open questions for the platform team

- Whether Meshy sublicenses API outputs to a platform's end users is
  unverified; it decides whether platform-held generation is cleanly
  licensed.
- Poly Pizza's licence split and API limits, and Freesound's commercial API
  terms, are unverified.
- The quota numbers above are recommendations awaiting adoption.
- How a creator records provenance (a file name, a manifest field) is not yet
  specified; until it is, keep a `CREDITS.md` next to the assets.
